- absent to present leakage: **2/9** (22.2%)

These two cells are not interchangeable with the rest of the matrix, or with each other.

A false `present` in a risk-adjustment pipeline becomes a submitted diagnosis, and a submitted diagnosis unsupported by the record is potential false-claims exposure -- a legal problem, not a metrics problem. A false `absent` is a missed condition: worse for the patient clinically, worse for the plan financially, and not a compliance event. Which system consumes the output decides which error you tune against, and reporting one F1 hides that the decision exists.

## Held-out errors, not fixed

Fixing these would convert them into development data and the held-out score would stop meaning anything. They are categorised instead, which is the more useful artefact: the category tells you what class of problem the system has, where a patch would only tell you that one sentence now works.

### [15] scope termination

> Status post myocardial infarction, now on aspirin and atorvastatin.

- `aspirin`: expected **present**, got **historical**
- `atorvastatin`: expected **present**, got **historical**

The `s/p` historical scope runs past 'now on' and swallows the current medications, marking active drugs as completed. A historical scope should terminate at a present-tense pivot, and 'now', 'currently' and 'today' are not in TERMINATION. This is the highest-severity of the three: it silently changes a live medication list into a historical one.

### [24] dictionary partition

> Intolerance to metformin with GI upset.

- missed: `[['metformin', 'allergy', 'present']]`
- spurious: `[['metformin', 'medication', 'present']]`

Any drug can be an allergen, but drugs live in MEDICATION_TERMS and allergens in ALLERGEN_TERMS, so 'intolerance to metformin' resolves to a medication rather than an allergy. The partition is the bug: allergen is a ROLE a concept plays in a context, not a separate vocabulary. Fixing it properly means making the allergy context promote any drug mention, which is a design change rather than a lexicon addition.

### [27] lexicon coverage

> Neither pneumonia nor heart failure explains the hypoxia.

- `pneumonia`: expected **absent**, got **present**
- `heart_failure`: expected **absent**, got **present**

'Neither X nor Y' is not in NEGATION_FORWARD. A pure coverage gap, and the characteristic failure of rule-based negation: it fails on phrasings nobody enumerated, and it fails SILENTLY by returning `present` -- the most dangerous possible default, because a missed negation becomes an asserted diagnosis.

## What a kappa would have caught, and is not reported

The gold sets have a single annotator: me. There is no second annotator, so no inter-annotator agreement, and the repeated-pass intra-annotator kappa the spec asks for is not fakeable in one session and is **not reported** rather than invented.

What that leaves unmeasured is specific, not vague. Several annotations in `gold/adversarial.py` are genuinely arguable -- most of all rule 3, that a negated family history stays `family` rather than becoming `absent`. A kappa would have quantified how consistently I applied that rule across 105 sentences written over a few hours. Without it, every F1 above inherits an unknown amount of annotator inconsistency, and the honest reading is that the held-out number has an error bar I cannot put a figure on.
